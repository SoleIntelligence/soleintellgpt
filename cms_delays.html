Editorial / Medicare coverage

When Will
Medicare Decide?

A complete request for a local coverage determination has no firm deadline from submission to a final decision.

Peter Sorensen, DPM · Revised 23 September 2026

01The timing gap

Medicare should set a deadline for local coverage decisions. Right now, a complete request can move through the process without a firm date for a final answer.

There are timing rules. A Medicare Administrative Contractor (MAC) generally reviews an LCD request for completeness within 60 calendar days. After the MAC publishes a proposed local coverage determination, it generally has 365 days to finalize or retire it. A final LCD needs at least 45 days of public notice before it takes effect. [1, 2]

The 365-day period starts when the proposed LCD is published. It does not start when the MAC receives a complete request. A reconsideration request can also go onto the MAC's waiting list. CMS has no firm deadline covering that full interval. [1, 2]

National coverage has a defined timeline

National coverage determinations (NCDs) follow a statutory timetable after CMS accepts a complete formal request. Local coverage uses the timing rules above. Those rules leave more uncertainty between the request and the final policy. [1–3]

A pending LCD does not automatically mean a treatment is unavailable. In the absence of a national policy, MACs may decide coverage locally. But an unanswered policy request leaves clinicians without a clear answer about how the contractor will treat that service across its jurisdiction. [3]

The evidence standard still matters

Skin substitutes show the cost of weak oversight. CMS reports that Medicare Part B spending for these products in the non-facility setting rose from $252 million in 2019 to more than $10 billion in 2024. CMS attributes most of that increase to higher payment rates and launch prices. It has also pointed to products with limited evidence of clinical value. [4, 5]

That evidence deserves scrutiny. Contractors should ask whether a product improves outcomes, for which patients, and compared with what. They should also explain what evidence is missing when they decline coverage.

Timeliness belongs in that same review. A long wait can be justified by a specific evidence problem. The reason and the expected decision date should be public.

Publish the full timeline

I would start with 4 dates for every complete LCD request: when the MAC accepts it, when it begins development, when it publishes a proposal, and when it reaches a final disposition. Publish requests placed on a waiting list and the reason for each delay.

Then report time to decision by contractor, including the longest-running requests. Set an outer deadline for a disposition and require a public explanation when a contractor misses it.

Keep the clinical evidence requirements. Put the full decision process on a measurable timeline.

That would give clinicians a clearer answer, and it would let CMS see where requests stall. If a request lacks adequate evidence, say so and identify the gap. If the evidence is ready for review, give the contractor a date to finish it.
