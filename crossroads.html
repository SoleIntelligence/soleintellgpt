Credentialing Crisis
Certification
Board Wars: ABFAS vs. ABPM
Two separate certifying bodies created confusion for hospitals, employers, and residents navigating credential requirements. CAQs added a third layer of complexity without uniform recognition.
Pipeline
Enrollment Decline & Match Anxiety
Several podiatric schools reported declining applicant pools. Residency positions grew but uneven distribution created geographic dead zones and program quality disparities.
Professional Identity
Scope
Scope of Practice Battles
Orthopedic encroachment on foot and ankle, uneven state-level legislation, and inconsistent hospital privileging left DPMs fighting for procedures they were fully trained to perform.
Economics
Reimbursement Erosion
CMS cuts and payer policy changes compressed podiatric margins. Routine care coding became a compliance minefield while high-value surgical reimbursements were reclassified or bundled.
Governance
CPME Accreditation Fights
Behind-the-scenes tension over CPME's authority and relationship with APMA raised questions about who governs podiatric education — and in whose interest.
A profession divided on its own credentials cannot speak with one voice to hospitals, legislatures, or patients.

Structural Reform
Certification
One Board Movement
APMA has formally engaged the "One Board" concept — unifying ABFAS and ABPM into a single certifying pathway. The 2026 HOD moved this from aspiration to active deliberation. Standardization reduces confusion and strengthens external credibility.
Certification
ABPM CAQ Discontinuation
The 2026 HOD included action to discontinue ABPM Certificates of Added Qualification — simplifying the credentialing landscape and reducing the administrative burden on residency graduates.
Legislative & Educational
Legislation
State-Level Scope Expansion
APMA's legislative arm is pursuing coordinated state-by-state strategies. Limb salvage, ankle arthroplasty, and reconstructive surgery privileging are priority battlegrounds with real momentum in key states.
Pipeline
Resident & Student Engagement
The APMA Emerging Leaders Program, HOD participation, and structured mentorship aim to rebuild civic investment in organized podiatry — connecting trainees to governance before they graduate.
Technology
AI & Operations
AI Integration in Practice
Early-adopter DPMs are deploying LLMs for surgical note generation, billing accuracy improvement, patient education, and research. The window to shape podiatry's AI narrative — before it's shaped for us — is open now.
Organized podiatry's power is latent. Residents who engage now inherit a profession shaped by their own hands.

Credentialing Resolution
Certification
A Unified Board Standard
If One Board succeeds, the profession presents a single, defensible credential to health systems and payers. Graduates will no longer face the confusion of dual pathways or employer unfamiliarity.
Scope
Expanded Surgical Authority
DPMs trained in ankle arthroplasty, limb salvage, and reconstructive procedures will increasingly hold privileges previously denied. Legislative wins will compound, state by state, over the decade.
Practice & Identity
Identity
The DPM Brand Clarified
A decade of organized effort — by HOD participants, academic researchers, and thought leaders — will shift public and hospital perception of the DPM from "foot doctor" to essential musculoskeletal specialist.
Technology
AI-Native Podiatric Practice
LLMs and computer vision will assist with preoperative imaging analysis, predictive outcomes modeling, and automated compliance documentation. DPMs who lead this transition will define the specialty's technological identity.
Workforce
Pipeline
Stabilized & Diversified Pipeline
Proactive engagement with pre-med students, community outreach, and GEO-optimized digital presence will attract the next generation. A clearer professional narrative makes recruitment easier and retention stronger.
The residents training today will preside over these outcomes. Indifference is a vote for the status quo.
