The Deskilling Turn

2026 is the year clinicians started naming AI skill-erosion as a top concern out loud. The answer isn't less AI. It's training AI as a competency — and keeping the doubt that makes you an active learner.

AI competence is now a clinical skill. The question isn't whether to use it — it's whether you keep the reps that make you good without it.
§01The shift

Adoption isn't the story anymore — it's the baseline. Roughly four in five physicians now report using AI in some form. What changed this spring is the other side of the ledger: deskilling — the slow erosion of independent clinical judgment when work gets habitually offloaded — now sits among the worries clinicians and patients carry into 2026. Not as a thought experiment. As a stated concern.

§02What "deskilling" actually means

Keep the definition observable: skill you stop practicing, you stop owning. The risk isn't the model — it's the habit of reaching for it before you've done the thinking, until the unaided version of the task quietly gets worse.

The counter

The honest limit: what we have is stated concern and early signal — surveys, sentiment, anecdote — not a measured decline in clinician skill. Treat deskilling as a risk to manage, not a verdict to repeat. And resist explaining a mechanism we can't yet prove.

§03Why podiatry should care first

Podiatry doesn't have to feel behind medicine on this. We're small enough to move fast and close enough to the patient to do it right — exactly the position from which to set the habit early. Use the tools, but protect the reps. You can't biohack the practice that builds a clinician.

§04The governance spine

The policy conversation is formalizing in step. A new health-AI policy index out of Mount Sinai sets out to track the moving regulatory and governance terrain — a sign that disclosure, oversight, and standards are coalescing from talking points into structure. Worth watching: it's the scaffolding the deskilling debate will hang on. Mount Sinai · verify

The take

AI commoditizes knowledge. It does not commoditize wisdom — and medicine runs on wisdom. Train the competency, keep the doubt, protect the reps. That's the whole brief.
