The Bill

Diabetes Foot Health Access and Modernization Act of 2026
HR 7905 / S 4070Two names, one bill. The House version and the Senate version.
March 12, 2026The day it was introduced.
Both partiesRepublicans and Democrats wrote it together.
What it actually does
1

Fix the shoe paperwork

Medicare: the government's health plan for people 65 and older, plus some younger people with disabilities.

Medicare pays for special diabetic shoes. These shoes help stop foot sores and amputations.

Right now, the paperwork to get those shoes is slow and clunky. This part of the bill cleans that up so patients get their shoes faster.

2

Match the anti-fraud rules

Fraud: cheating the system to get paid for things that didn't happen.

The government already has standard rules to catch fraud on normal doctor services.

Right now the diabetic shoe benefit uses its own separate rules. This part makes the shoe rules match the normal doctor rules. Same guardrails, less confusion.

3

Pay podiatrists fairly under Medicaid

Medicaid: the government's health plan for people with low income. (Different from Medicare.)

In some states, Medicaid pays podiatrists less than other doctors for the same work.

This part says: pay them the same. The word for equal treatment is parity.

Honest note: this idea is not new. It has been sitting in an older bill called the HELLPP Act for years. The new bill just folds it in. Knowing that keeps you from thinking it's a fresh win.
Why a student should care

You have the most years to lose or gain

An attending might practice 10 more years. You'll practice 30+. These rules hit you the hardest and the longest.

2 of the 3 parts are billing and paperwork

School spends almost no time on this. Learn how billing works now and you start your career ahead of most people around you.

This is how the profession really works

A small group of people writing one bill can shape your whole career. That's why APMA (the American Podiatric Medical Association, the group that lobbies for podiatrists) (Peter here - and your state associations!)matters. Pay attention to who's in the room.
