The Lapidus Procedure

First TMT arthrodesis for hypermobile hallux valgus — a resident's framework

Forefoot Reconstruction 1st Ray Stabilization Arthrodesis Hallux Valgus
🔹 What This Is

The Lapidus bunionectomy is an arthrodesis of the first tarsometatarsal (TMT) joint combined with soft-tissue correction at the MTP. It addresses hallux valgus at its root cause — metatarsus primus varus driven by first ray hypermobility — rather than just the distal deformity.

🔹 Why It Matters
Surgical Rationale
Hypermobile 1st ray → distal procedures have higher recurrence. TMT fusion eliminates the mobile segment driving deformity.
Biomechanical Upshot
Corrects IMA, HAV angle, and sagittal plane elevation of the 1st ray simultaneously — multi-plane correction in one joint.
Resident Relevance
Commonly performed in reconstructive forefoot cases. Understanding fixation constructs and correction vectors is essential for assisting effectively.
Outcomes Signal
Literature supports durable recurrence prevention in properly selected patients vs. metatarsal osteotomies when hypermobility is the driver.
🔹 The Framework — Operative Sequence
1
Joint preparation
Dorsomedial approach to 1st TMT. Cartilage resection with flat cuts preserving subchondral bone. Goal: bleeding surfaces that will fuse. Technique varies — saw vs. curette vs. combination.
↓
2
Deformity correction (the key move)
Plantarflex 1st ray + adduct metatarsal to close the IMA. Provisional K-wire fixation. Fluoroscopic check: IMA, HAV angle, sesamoid position, sagittal alignment.
↓
3
Definitive fixation
Hardware construct applied per surgeon preference (plate ± lag screw, crossed screws, staple). Compression across the fusion site is paramount.
↓
4
1st MTP soft-tissue balancing
Medial capsulorrhaphy ± lateral soft-tissue release. Degree of release depends on residual MTP deformity after proximal correction.
↓
5
Closure + post-op protocol
NWB × 4–6 wks typically, then transition. Bone healing confirmation before full WB progression. Hardware removal rarely needed unless symptomatic.
🔹 Key Concepts
IMA (intermetatarsal angle) → Normal <9°. HV deformity correlates with elevated IMA. Lapidus directly closes IMA at its origin — the TMT joint.
1st ray hypermobility → Sagittal AND transverse instability at the TMT. Clinical test: excessive dorsiflexion-plantarflexion excursion with lateral loading. Key indication for Lapidus over distal osteotomy.
DMAA (distal metatarsal articular angle) → If significantly elevated, proximal correction alone may not normalize articular alignment — consider adjunct distal procedure.
Sesamoid position → Tibial sesamoid subluxation graded on AP view. Reduction of sesamoids under the metatarsal head is a key correction endpoint — not just angle reduction.
Sagittal plane consideration → Avoid dorsiflexing the 1st ray during correction. Elevated 1st ray → transfer lesion under 2nd metatarsal. Plantarflexion during correction maintains propulsion mechanics.
Length preservation → Resection must be minimal to avoid shortening. 1st MT shortening → metatarsalgia + transfer lesion. Flat cut technique minimizes length loss vs. biplanar cut.
🔩 Hardware Constructs
Dorsomedial plate ± lag screw
Current workhorse. Locking or non-locking. Low-profile designs reduce irritation. Plantar lag screw adds compression in orthogonal plane. High rotational stability.
Crossed lag screws
Historical standard. Two screws in divergent configuration. Technically demanding to achieve compression. Less rigid vs. plate construct — longer NWB often advised.
Nitinol staples
Shape-memory compression across fusion site. Lower profile than plates. Often used as adjunct or in minimally invasive variants. Less fixation strength as standalone.
Plantar plate + screw
Anatomic plantar positioning resists dorsiflexion forces. Theoretical mechanical advantage for sagittal plane stability. Technically more demanding approach.

🔹 Pitfalls / High-Yield Mistakes
⚠
Dorsiflexion malposition of 1st ray
Fusing in dorsiflexion → elevated 1st ray → transfer metatarsalgia under 2nd head. Plantarflexion moment during correction is intentional, not aggressive.
⚠
Underresecting joint cartilage
Non-union risk rises if subchondral bone not adequately exposed. Cartilage remnants inhibit osseous bridging. Complete debridement to bleeding surface is non-negotiable.
⚠
Excessive metatarsal shortening
Too much bone resection → shortened 1st ray → iatrogenic lesser metatarsal overload. Keep resection minimal; work with flat cuts.
⚠
Ignoring DMAA / MTP joint pathology
Correcting IMA without addressing a significantly elevated DMAA or arthritic MTP → residual frontal plane deformity at the toe. Plan distal adjunct preoperatively.
⚠
Premature weight bearing
Hardware failure and non-union are real. Fusion biology takes time. NWB protocols exist for a reason — rushing WB for patient comfort is a setup for hardware compromise.
⚡ Controversies / Active Debates
Does every HV patient need hypermobility to justify Lapidus?
Traditionalist: reserve for hypermobile / severe IMA Modern: Lapidus as universal HV correction regardless of mobility
Optimal fixation construct?
Dorsomedial plate + lag screw (most common) Plantar plate for biomechanical superiority
Early weight bearing post-Lapidus — safe?
Strict NWB × 6 wks (traditional) Immediate / early protected WB with rigid plate constructs (growing literature)
Minimally invasive Lapidus?
Open remains gold standard for deformity control MIS/percutaneous variants emerging with comparable early outcomes
📈 Current Trends
↑
Early WB protocols — improved plate rigidity enabling WBAT in boot sooner than historical NWB × 6 wks; growing evidence base
↑
Low-profile locking plate systems — dedicated Lapidus plates with anatomic contouring reducing hardware irritation and revision rates
↑
Lapidus as primary correction — trend toward broader use beyond classic hypermobility indication, driven by durable recurrence prevention data
↑
MIS / percutaneous Lapidus — emerging techniques aiming to reduce soft-tissue disruption; limited long-term follow-up data as of now
↑
3D imaging preoperative planning — weightbearing CT (WBCT) for sagittal plane assessment and identifying multiplanar deformity components
🔹 Pearls
💎
The Lapidus is a 3D correction — think transverse + sagittal. A flat radiograph only tells part of the story. WBCT is increasingly your friend preoperatively.
💎
Sesamoid reduction is the functional endpoint — not just angle normalization. Confirm under fluoro that tibial sesamoid is tracking under the head.
💎
The hypermobility exam is clinical and subjective. No validated threshold. Develop your own consistent assessment technique and use it every patient.
💎
Plantarflexion during correction is intentional. Overcorrecting sagittal plane elevation while closing IMA is the move — not a side effect.
💎
When assisting: watch the provisional K-wire placement and correction hold. That moment determines everything downstream — the hardware just locks it in.
