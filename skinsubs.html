The Skin Substitute Debacle

A forty-fold spending increase, a billion-dollar fraud sentence, a rate cut that kills a business model on contact, and a Christmas Eve reversal that nobody saw coming. How Medicare collapsed a ten-billion-dollar market in ninety days — without ever touching the coverage rules.

§ 01The Mechanism

How a reimbursement rule turned into a market.

Key term ASP+6%. Average Sales Price plus six percent. Manufacturers self-report pricing; Medicare pays that price plus a six percent margin to the clinician who applies the product.

Under the pre-2026 payment structure, Medicare reimbursed most skin substitutes under the ASP+6% methodology used for biologicals. The design was coherent for drugs with competitive markets and disciplined launch prices. It was not coherent for a product category where manufacturers could set launch prices above $2,000 per square centimeter, collect ASP+6% on every application, and pay clinicians "spread" arrangements that functioned as kickbacks by any honest reading.

The incentive ran one direction. Higher list price meant higher reimbursement meant higher clinician margin meant higher volume. A 2023 OIG investigation found that nearly half of the relevant billing codes lacked required ASP pricing submissions, leaving CMS to rely on inflated benchmarks. The system had no brake.

By 2024, Medicare was spending over $10 billion annually on skin substitutes — more than 15% of all Part B drug spending — while beneficiary volume had only doubled. The delta was pricing and overutilization, not patient need.

§ 02The Criminal Response

DOJ made skin substitutes a featured category.

The 2025 National Health Care Fraud Takedown charged 324 defendants across 50 federal districts. Wound care was not a footnote. It was headline copy. What follows is a partial roster.

§ 03The Regulatory Pincer

CMS chose payment methodology over coverage restriction.

The Rule CY 2026 Medicare Physician Fee Schedule Final Rule (CMS-1832-F). Finalized October 31, 2025. Payment policy effective January 1, 2026.

The October 31 final rule reclassified most skin substitutes from biologicals to "incident-to supplies" and set a uniform payment rate across physician office and hospital outpatient settings. The CAA of 2021 had already required ASP submissions; the new rule stopped pretending those submissions would discipline pricing.

Products licensed under PHSA §351 remain on ASP methodology. Everything else falls under the flat rate. Reimbursement is now tied strictly to applied surface area — no payment for discarded or unused material. Measurement precision, previously a clinical formality, is now the audit vector.

Eighteen months that collapsed a market.

§ 05The Christmas Eve Reversal

CMS didn't need the LCDs once the payment side was done.

Withdrawn LCDs Skin Substitute Grafts / Cellular and Tissue-Based Products for the Treatment of DFUs and VLUs. Would have limited covered products, required duration of standard care, vascular assessment, and capped applications at 8.

The withdrawn LCDs would have accomplished by coverage restriction what the PFS accomplished by payment math. With the rate cut live, the products that depended on inflated ASP for viability exit the market regardless of whether they remain "covered." This is the tell.

AATB and manufacturers are lobbying for rate increases. APMA formally opposed the payment proposal but won on arthrodesis RVUs — CPT 28755 jumped from 4.88 to 7.50 wRVU — as partial consolation. The trade association response continues, but the economics are settled for CY 2026.

Three MACs — Novitas, CGS, and First Coast — kept their own skin substitute policies active. Providers in those jurisdictions are not on the same glide path as the rest of the country.

§ 06What This Means

Four lessons for the next CMS cycle.

01
Payment methodology is a policy lever, not a technicality.
CMS used rate reform to accomplish what LCDs could not — without the political cost of coverage denials. When the economics change, the prescribing patterns change. The LCDs became redundant on the same day the PFS was finalized.
02
Specialties are judged by their worst billers.
A handful of podiatrists and NPs running industrial-scale schemes reshape how an entire specialty is perceived by OIG, DOJ, and Congress. When the OIG report landed, "podiatry" appeared in the enforcement framing. The reputational externality is not hypothetical.
03
Measurement is now the audit surface.
Applied cm² determines payment. Estimated wound areas that were previously rounding errors are now direct margin hits and audit flags. Objective, repeatable measurement — including digital or AI-assisted workflows — is no longer optional for wound-focused practices.
04
Enforcement is not receding with reimbursement.
DOJ stood up a Health Care Fraud Data Fusion Center in 2026. Wound Care Initiative remains a named priority. Pre-2026 billing patterns are still on the table. Providers with significant skin substitute volume in the ASP+6% era should know where their documentation stands.
